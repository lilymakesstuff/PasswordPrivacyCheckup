1. Account Safety Checks

  • On this computer

    Consumer Reports Security Planner ↗

    When you're done, if you would like to save your report, paste the link here and scan the code with your phone.

    Download QR code

  • On your phone
    I have a Google account

    You must have a Google account (Gmail, Google Drive, etc.) that is your personal account to do this checkup.

    1. Navigate to your favorite web browser app.
    2. Go to Google and sign into your account.
    3. Go to the Security Checkup page at https://myaccount.google.com/security-checkup/.
    4. Psst. You don’t have to type it in; you can tap your account image, tap the Settings wheel, and then scroll down to Privacy and Security.
    5. Look at the shields at the top of the page.
    6. If your shield is green, you’re good to go! You can close the browser. Congrats on being a security expert!
    7. If your shield is yellow or red, scroll down and look at what steps Google recommends you complete. You can do them now, or later.
    8. Let someone know if you have trouble with any of the recommended actions.
    9. Never show staff your passwords or confidential information.
    I have an iPhone (iOS 16 or later)

    You must have an iPhone (running iOS 16 or later) that is yours to do this checkup. Android devices can use the Google checkup instructions listed above.

    1. Open the Settings app on your phone (you can also search for Settings in the search bar).
    2. Tap “Privacy and Security”.
    3. Scroll down and tap Safety Check.
    4. It will ask for your password, face ID, or fingerprint. Type it in.
    5. Tap “Manage Sharing and Access”. Don’t hit the other option, it will reset your phone!
    6. Take a look at the people and apps that you’re sharing your data with. If there are any apps you never use anymore, or people you don’t talk to, it might be best to remove them. This is up to you.
    7. Tap Done.
    8. Let someone know if you have trouble with any of the recommended actions.
    9. Never show staff your passwords or confidential information.

2. Spam Protection

  • On this computer

    Google Phishing Quiz ↗

    These ten questions will test your ability to identify phishing attempts.

  • On your phone
    I have an iPhone
    1. Open the Settings app on your phone (you can also search for Settings in the search bar).
    2. Tap Apps.
    3. Tap Phone.
    4. Scroll down to Call Filtering.
    5. From here, you can turn on your spam filter.
    6. If you want to see your Spam list, tap the Filter button, then tap Spam.
    7. Tip! In the Phone app, there are two views: Classic, and Unified. In Classic it shows in the Recents tab, and in the Unified view it shows in Calls.
    8. You’re done!
    I have an Android phone
    1. Android phones are all a little different, so ask someone how these instructions will work for your phone if needed.
    2. Open the Phone app.
    3. Find the menu. It will look like a cog, or three dots or three lines in the corner.
    4. Select Settings.
    5. Tap Caller ID & spam (or Spam Protection).
    6. Turn on Filter spam calls if you choose.
    7. You can also toggle settings such as See Caller and Spam ID if you want.
    8. You’re done! Exit the Phone app.

3. Removing Logged-in Devices

  • On this computer

    Have I Been Pwned ↗

    Heads up! If your email has been in a breach, it doesn't mean your account has been hacked. It does mean you might want to change your password.

    This website has many major data breaches listed, allowing you to check if your email has been compromised.

  • On your phone
    I have a Google account

    You must have a Google account (Gmail, Google Drive, etc.) that is your personal account to do this checkup.

    1. On your phone, open your favorite web browser and sign into Google.
    2. Go to https://myaccount.google.com/device-activity ↗. You can also tap your account image, tap Manage your Google Account, and select Security.
    3. Find “Your devices” and select Manage all devices.
    4. Look through the list. Check the device names and when they were last active.
    5. If you see a device you don’t use or don’t recognize, select it, then select Sign out.
    6. If it is your device, you can always sign in with it later!
    7. You’re done!
    8. Let someone know if you have trouble with any of these steps.
    9. Never show staff your passwords or confidential information.
    I have an iPhone

    You must have an iPhone that is yours to do this checkup. Android devices can use the Google checkup instructions listed above.

    1. Open the Settings app on your iPhone.
    2. Tap your name at the very top.
    3. Scroll down to see a list of devices signed in to your Apple Account.
    4. Tap a device you don’t use or don’t recognize.
    5. Tap Remove from Account, then confirm.
    6. Tip! The device labeled “This iPhone” is the one in your hand. Don’t remove that one.
    7. You’re done!
    8. Let someone know if you have trouble with any of these steps.
    9. Never show staff your passwords or confidential information.

4. Setting up MFA on your email

  • On this computer

    2FA Directory (US) ↗

    Look up your two or three most used sites and see if they offer MFA. If they are, they're safer! If they don't, consider switching if you have lots of personal information on the account.

  • On your phone
    I use Gmail or another Google service

    You must have a Google account (Gmail, Google Drive, etc.) that is your personal account to do this checkup.

    1. Go to https://myaccount.google.com/security ↗ on your phone and sign in.
    2. Under “How you sign in to Google,” select 2-Step Verification.
    3. Select Get started and follow the prompts. Google will ask you to confirm your phone number or use Google prompts on your phone.
    4. Turn it on when asked.
    I use iCloud Mail or an Apple Account

    You must have an Apple account (iPhone, etc.) that is your personal account to do this checkup. You must have an iCloud account that is your personal account to do this checkup.

    1. Open the Settings app on your iPhone and tap your name at the top.
    2. Tap Sign-In & Security.
    3. Tap Two-Factor Authentication.
    4. If it says “On,” you’re all set.
    5. If not, tap Turn On Two-Factor Authentication and follow the prompts.
    I use Outlook or Hotmail

    You must have a Microsoft account that is your personal account to do this checkup.

    1. Go to https://account.microsoft.com/security ↗ and sign in.
    2. Select Advanced security options.
    3. Find Two-step verification and select Turn on.
    4. Follow the prompts to choose how you want to get your codes.
    I use Yahoo Mail

    You must have a Yahoo account that is your personal account to do this checkup.

    1. Sign into Yahoo, then select your account image and choose Account info.
    2. Select Account security.
    3. Turn on Two-step verification and follow the prompts.
    I use another email service
    1. Look for “Security,” “Account Settings,” or “Sign-in” in your account settings, then search for “2-step,” “two-factor,” or “MFA.”
    2. If you can’t find it, ask us!

You can choose which activities to do. Ask us if you get stuck, and never show staff your passwords or confidential information.
