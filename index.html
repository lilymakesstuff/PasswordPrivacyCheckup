Password and Privacy Checkup

Follow the guide that matches your device or account, and check off activities as you complete them. You only need to do what is relevant to you, and you may have some of these already done.

1. Check up on your account security

  • On this computer

    Consumer Reports Security Planner ↗

    When you're done, if you would like to save your report, paste the link here and scan the code with your phone.

    Download QR code

  • On your phone
    I have a Google account

    Open your browser and sign in to your personal Google account. Go to Google Security Checkup ↗. A green shield means you’re all set. If it’s yellow or red, review Google’s recommended steps and ask for help if needed.

    I have an iPhone (iOS 16 or later)
    1. Open Settings, tap Privacy & Security, then Safety Check.
    2. Authenticate, then tap Manage Sharing & Access. Do not choose Emergency Reset.
    3. Review the people and apps with access to your information. Remove access only if you choose, then tap Done.

2. Spam protection

  • On this computer

    Google Phishing Quiz ↗

    [Description placeholder]

  • On your phone
    I have an iPhone
    1. Open Settings, then tap Apps and Phone.
    2. Scroll to Call Filtering and turn on the spam filter if you want to use it.
    3. To view spam calls, open the Phone app, tap Filter, then Spam. The tab may be called Recents or Calls depending on your view.
    I have an Android phone
    1. Open the Phone app, then its menu (often a gear, three dots, or three lines) and tap Settings.
    2. Tap Caller ID & spam or Spam protection.
    3. Turn on Filter spam calls or other options if you want. Android menus vary, so ask for help if needed.

3. Remove devices you no longer use

  • On this computer

    Have I Been Pwned ↗

    Heads up! If your email has been in a breach, it doesn't mean your account has been hacked. It does mean you might want to change your password.

    [Description placeholder]

  • On your phone
    I have a Google account
    1. Open your browser and sign in to Google. Go to Your devices ↗, or open your account’s Security settings and choose Manage all devices.
    2. Review device names and last activity. Choose an unfamiliar or unused device, then select Sign out.
    I have an iPhone
    1. Open Settings and tap your name.
    2. Tap an unfamiliar or unused device in the list, then tap Remove from Account and confirm. Do not remove This iPhone.

4. Set up two-step sign-in

  • On this computer

    2FA Directory (US) ↗

    [Description placeholder]

  • On your phone
    I use Gmail or another Google service

    Open Google Security settings ↗. Under How you sign in to Google, choose 2-Step Verification, then follow the prompts to turn it on.

    I use iCloud Mail or an Apple Account

    On your iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Sign-In & Security and Two-Factor Authentication. If it’s off, tap to turn it on and follow the prompts.

    I use Outlook or Hotmail

    Open Microsoft account security ↗, sign in, and choose Advanced security options. Find Two-step verification, turn it on, and follow the prompts.

    I use Yahoo Mail

    Sign in to Yahoo, open your account image and Account info, then Account security. Turn on Two-step verification and follow the prompts.

    I use another email service

    Look in your account settings for Security, Sign-in, or Two-factor authentication. Ask for help if you can’t find it.

You can choose which activities to do. Ask us if you get stuck, and never show staff your passwords or confidential information.
